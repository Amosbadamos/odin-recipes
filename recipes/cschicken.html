Crime Scene Chicken

Chicken

  • 2 (4 1/2 pound) whole chicken
  • 4 teaspoons kosher salt

Wet Rub

  • ⅔ cup gochujang(Korean chili paste)
  • ½ cup ketchup
  • 4 tablespoons honey
  • 4 tablespoons soy sauce
  • ½ cup rice vinegar
  • 2 teaspoons garlic powder
  • 2 teaspoons onion powder
  • 2 teaspoons sesame oil

Blood Sauce

  • reserved wet rub
  • ⅔ cup water
  • 2 teaspoons cornstarch
  • 2 tablespoons water

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 450 degrees F (235 degrees C). Line a baking sheet with aluminum foil, and lightly oil the foil.
  2. Place the chicken on a cutting board, and use a sharp knife to make deep cuts down the length of each leg down to the joint. Make two or three deep cuts across these cuts, and then do the same thing into the side of each thigh. Make a deep cut through the center of each breast, down to the rib cage, and then make three or four cuts across this cut. Additional cuts can be made wherever you think they will fit. All these cuts can be made in a uniform pattern, or offset slightly for a more random “violent” look. Season the chicken inside and out with the salt and set aside momentarily.
  3. Transfer chicken into the large bowl with wet rub, and turn back-and-forth until evenly coated. Using a pastry brush, brush wet rub into all the cuts, and into every nook and cranny.
  4. Transfer chicken onto the prepared sheet pan. Use a clean pastry brush to brush any excess wet rub from the bowl onto the chicken.
  5. Roast chicken in the preheated oven until an instant-read thermometer inserted into the thickest part of the thigh reads at least 155 degrees F (68 degrees C), an hour to an hour and 10 minutes, depending on the size of the chicken.
  6. Meanwhile, place reserved wet rub on the stove and add 1/3 cup water. Stir the cornstarch together with 1 tablespoon of cold water, and set aside. Bring sauce to a simmer over medium high heat, and whisk cornstarch mixture into the sauce. Reduce heat to low, and cook, stirring, for 1 minute.
  7. Keep in a warm spot while chicken roasts. Sauce texture can be adjusted with more water if needed. Remove chicken from the oven and let it rest for about five minutes.
  8. Carefully place chicken onto a serving platter. Pour any pan drippings into the blood sauce, and reheat over medium-low heat if necessary. Drizzle and drip sauce onto the chicken and platter to create a “crime scene,” adding “weapons” to the platter if desired. Serve chicken with additional sauce once plated.
